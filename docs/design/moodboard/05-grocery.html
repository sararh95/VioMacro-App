Board 05 · /grocery and /pantry

The only screen used
standing up, one-handed.

Everything else in VioMacro is used sitting down. The grocery list is used in a supermarket, holding a trolley, in bad light. That single fact should drive every decision here — and it is the screen where reviewers of other apps are most emotional about the details.

What we take from whom

  • AnyListPeople reorder categories to match their own walk through the store, then finish the shop far faster. The order is personal, not universal.
  • The KitchnThe beloved micro-interaction: a checked item fades for a second — long enough to undo a mis-tap — then disappears, leaving only what is left.
  • Plan to EatThe list must arrive from the plan without being asked twice. The handoff is the feature.
  • FlippSimple navigation beats feature depth on this screen. People praise it for removing clutter, not adding power.
  • Your blueprintThe list should be inventory-aware — subtract what is already in the pantry. Nobody studied here does this.

Concept A — the aisle walk

/grocery Big targets, one thumb, nothing above the fold but the list

Design bet: in the shop, the sidebar full of plan metadata is worthless. Move everything contextual into a collapsed header and give the whole screen to items. Rows are tall, targets are wide, and the counter tells you how much is left rather than how much there was.

The three interactions that matter

  • Tap anywhere on the row to check it. Not a small checkbox — the entire row, minimum 56 px tall.
  • The undo window. A checked item strikes through and dims but stays in place for about 1.2 seconds, then slides out to a collapsed "done" group. This is the specific behaviour people describe as satisfying, and the reason it works is that a mis-tap in a supermarket is common.
  • The counter counts down. "14 left" is motivating in a way "9 of 23 complete" is not.

Reorder the aisles

The five categories are currently fixed in source order. AnyList's most-praised feature is letting people drag them into their own store's layout. That single setting turns a list into a route. It also fixes a latent bug: an item whose aisle is not one of the five hardcoded names would render nowhere at all.

Concept B — fixing the handoff

broken today Plan → list, with a visible arrival

What happens now: you press "Add to List" on the planner, a browser alert says "Added 12 items", you walk to the grocery page — and they are not there. The generator writes straight to the database and the grocery page never asks for fresh data. Only a full browser reload reveals them.

  • The confirmation is inline and carries a link, not a blocking alert the user must dismiss before they can act on it.
  • It reports what it skipped — items already in the pantry, and any meal whose recipe could not be resolved. Today a failed generation shows nothing at all, because the code only handles the success branch.
  • Newly added items pulse once on arrival so the user can see the handoff actually happened.
  • Manual items are never touched by a regeneration, and they carry a small marker so that promise is visible.

Concept C — the pantry finally does something

/pantry An inventory that changes what you buy and cook

Your blueprint calls this "a critical, underserved user need" and "a powerful retention driver". Today nothing outside the Pantry page reads the pantry at all. It is an honest prototype with a Coming Soon banner — but it is the single highest-value unbuilt connection in the product, and no competitor studied here has it.

Delete the fake expiry first

Every pantry item is currently stamped as expiring in exactly seven days — rice, milk, tinned beans, all of them. Of every invented value in the app this is the most dangerous, because an expiry date is something a person acts on: they throw food away, or they trust something spoiled. Leave it empty unless the user sets it, and only show the "Use soon" group when there is a real date to show.

The two connections that make the pantry real

  1. Into the planner. Generation prefers recipes whose ingredients are already in stock. This is the anti-food-waste, anti-decision-fatigue feature your blueprint describes.
  2. Into the grocery list. Generation subtracts what you own. "Skipped 4 — already at home" is a line that makes people trust the app immediately.

Until both exist, the Coming Soon banner is the right call. But this board is what it should become, and the quick-add prototype already collects the right data for it.

Small things that people notice

DetailWhy it matters in a shop
Stable sort orderThe list currently has no ordering, so items can reshuffle between reloads. Nothing destroys trust faster than a list that moves while you are reading it.
Fraction-aware quick add"1/2 cup rice" is currently read as 1 cup. A correct parser already exists elsewhere in the codebase — it is simply not the one this field uses.
Units survive"2 kg flour" currently drops the kg. Quantities without units are useless at a shelf.
Offline-tolerant tickingSupermarket signal is bad. Ticks queue locally and sync when signal returns, rather than failing.
Sharing, when it existsReal collaborator avatars are one of the most valuable elements this screen could carry. Which is exactly why the current decorative fake ones should go — they spend the credibility before the feature earns it.
