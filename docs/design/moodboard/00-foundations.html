Board 00 · Foundations

The system underneath every screen.

Keep the violet — it is already your identity and it is genuinely distinctive in a category full of green and orange. Everything else here is new: a data palette that stops the brand colour doing two jobs, a type pairing with more character, and six laws drawn straight from what reviewers complain about.

Six laws

Each one exists because reviewers of the studied apps complained about its absence.

LawWhat it means on screenWhere it came from
1. Count the tapsEvery daily job carries a target action count. A prettier layout that costs two more taps loses.MacroFactor's measured logging study
2. Never move under a fingerReserve space before content loads. Skeletons match the exact height of the real thing. Nothing reflows after paint.The single loudest MyFitnessPal complaint
3. Empty is a state, not a gapEvery list, chart and card has a designed empty state that names the next action. No zeros pretending to be data.Your audit: 17 places currently invent a value
4. Two questions to valueAsk at most two things before the user sees something useful. Everything else is asked in context, later.Onboarding drop-off benchmarks
5. One tap to undoAny destructive or reversible action fades for a beat before it commits. No confirmation dialogs for things that can simply be undone.The grocery check-off pattern reviewers love
6. Say the unitEvery number carries its unit, and the unit follows the user's setting. Never a hardcoded suffix.Your audit: metric is default and broken in four places

Why this matters more than it looks

Today Dashboard.tsx paints protein in the brand violet. So violet means both "this is VioMacro" and "this is protein" — and the eye cannot tell a primary button from a macro bar at a glance. Giving each macro its own hue frees violet to mean one thing: the thing you should tap.

Type

Two faces, three roles. Sora for display gives the numbers a slightly geometric, instrument-panel quality that suits a metabolic tool without tipping into sci-fi. Inter carries body text because it disappears, which is what body text should do. IBM Plex Mono handles anything that lines up in a column.

RoleFaceUsed forNotes
DisplaySora 600/700Page titles, big numbers, card headingsTight tracking at large sizes (-0.025em)

The tabular-numerals rule

Any number that can change while the user watches — a calorie counter ticking up, a water total, a weight — must use tabular figures. Without them the digits are different widths, so the number jiggles as it updates. That is Law 2 breaking in miniature, and it is the kind of thing people feel without being able to name.

Spacing & shape

  • 4px base scale: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64. Nothing between.
  • Radius means depth: 4px for inputs and chips, 8px for cards, 16px for sheets and modals. A consistent radius ladder does more for perceived quality than any single flourish.
  • Layout owns spacing: flex/grid with gap. No per-element margins that collapse or double.
  • Thumb zone: on mobile the primary action of every screen sits in the bottom third. Nothing important lives in the top corners.

Motion

The research is blunt about this: motion that confirms is loved, motion that decorates is noise. Three uses only.

  • Confirm (120ms): a tick lands, a chip fills, a button depresses. Fast enough to feel like cause and effect.
  • Undo window (1200ms): a checked item fades but stays put, so a mis-tap is recoverable, then leaves. This is the interaction reviewers single out by name.
  • Reveal (200ms, ease-out): a sheet rising, a section expanding. Never on first paint — the page should arrive already there.

Everything respects prefers-reduced-motion. And there is a fourth rule: never animate a number's container. Animate the fill, not the box.

Two things to delete everywhere

Pulsing "Live" badges

A pulse is a promise that something is happening right now. Nothing is. Remove them until the adaptive engine exists, then use the pulse only while it is genuinely recalculating.

Decorative avatar stacks

The grocery header currently shows two faces and a +2 badge. That is the universal symbol for "other people are here". Nobody is. Delete it until sharing ships — then it becomes one of the most valuable elements on the screen.
