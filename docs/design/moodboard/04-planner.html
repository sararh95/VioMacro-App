you can pick things up and move.

This is the screen your blueprint bets the company on — the modular "building blocks" idea that fights food monotony by mixing a small set of prepped components into many meals. It is also your largest file at 1,690 lines, and the place where a save can silently duplicate a week.

The details that make it work

  • Batched meals share one tint across the days they cover, so the batch reads as a single object spanning the week rather than four identical entries.
  • Each day's calorie meter sits under its column. Short days are visible without reading a number.
  • The gap-filler is one tap and suggests recipes that fit the remaining macros — this already exists as GapSolverModal.
  • Empty slots are inviting, not alarming — a dashed outline that means "drop something here", not an error.
  • Save is a single explicit action. It must be idempotent: double-clicking it currently creates two plans for the same week.

Concept B — batch cooking as a first-class object

differentiator Prep once, eat four times

Nobody studied here does this. Every competitor treats "the same lunch on four days" as four separate entries. Your blueprint calls batch cooking a direct intervention against food monotony — which means the batch should be an object with its own card, not a repetition.

  • One cook session, scheduled. The batch has a prep day, and that prep day can carry a reminder.
  • Ingredients aggregate. A batch of four contributes one grocery line, which is exactly what a person needs at the shop.
  • Breaking a batch is reversible and does not lose the individual days' macros.
  • Cook mode knows it is a batch and scales the quantities in each step.

Concept C — generation that respects choices

re-roll Lock what you like, regenerate the rest

The failure mode to avoid: generate a week, love two meals, hate five, and have no option except regenerating everything and losing the two good ones. Eat This Much solved this with locking, and it is the difference between a toy and a tool.

The precondition nobody can skip

Generation needs recipes. Today an empty library silently generates a week from demo recipes the user does not own, producing meals whose recipe IDs point at nothing. The honest design blocks generation and sends the user to the library — annoying for one screen, correct forever after.

Two views that should not exist yet

Day and Month both render a "Coming Soon" construction screen. That is honest, and honest is better than fake — but a view switcher offering two dead options costs a tap and teaches people the app is unfinished. Suggestion: hide Day and Month until they exist. When Day ships, make it the mobile default, because a seven-column grid on a phone is a compromise and a single day is not.
