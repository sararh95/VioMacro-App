Board 03 · /recipes, /recipes/import, detail, cook mode

The library is the fuel.
Everything else runs on it.

Nothing in VioMacro works without recipes — the planner cannot generate, the grocery list cannot resolve, the pantry has nothing to match. Which is exactly why the current fallback to eleven demo recipes is so damaging: it lets the whole app appear to work on an empty library.

What we take from whom

  • Paprika4.9 stars across ~52,000 reviews for one reason: it does not tell you what to eat. People own their library. Never overwrite, never seed, never suggest deletion.
  • CroutonAn Apple Design Award for its cook mode. One step at a time, large type, timers detected inline from the text of the step itself.
  • Samsung FoodImport from anywhere is the acquisition feature. The strongest free cross-platform option, and import is why.
  • Category trend2026 apps favour data over glossy magazine photography. Macros on the card beat a bigger photo.
  • Paprika (warning)Reviewers say the interface feels dated. Ownership without craft only holds people so long.

Concept A — the library grid

/recipes Macro-forward cards, photo optional

Design bet: most recipes a real person adds have no good photo — they come from a phone snap of a cookbook page or a PDF. A layout that assumes beautiful photography will look broken on a real library. So the card leads with the title and macros, and treats the image as a background wash when one exists.

Card anatomy

  • Title first, always readable — never overlaid on an unpredictable photo without a scrim.
  • A three-bar macro signature under each card. At a glance you can spot the high-protein recipes across the whole grid without reading a single number.
  • No photo? No problem. The card falls back to a tinted panel derived from the recipe's dominant macro. It looks intentional rather than broken.
  • Favourite is a corner tap with an optimistic fill that reverses visibly if the save fails.

Delete on sight

The fallback to demo recipes when the library is empty or when the read fails. An empty library should say it is empty. A failed read should say it failed and offer Retry. Right now both render as a stocked library of food the user does not own — and they can add it to a real plan.

Concept B — import as one gesture

/recipes/import Drop → parse → check → save

The promise in your blueprint: a multi-minute manual task becomes one click. The mechanic that matters is the review step — the AI proposes, the human confirms. Never save silently, and never fill blanks with invented numbers.

Two fixes baked into this concept

Fields the parser could not find stay empty and say so — the current code hardcodes 30 minutes and 4 servings on every import, which also means every imported recipe permanently matches the "Under 30m" filter. And every PDF import currently shares one stock photo pulled from an external site; here a missing image is simply a tinted panel.

Add the missing source

Your blueprint's headline import feature is paste a URL. The build has image and PDF but not links — and a link is the single most common way a person meets a recipe. The drop zone above accepts all three, so adding it later is a parser change, not a redesign.

Concept C — cook mode

award-winning pattern One step, big type, timer where the time is mentioned

Why this pattern won: in a kitchen the phone is on a counter, hands are wet, and the screen is a metre away. Every design decision follows from those three facts.

Simmer the rice for 12 minutes, covered.
Start 12:00 timer detected from the step text
Ingredients for this step — 1 cup rice, 2 cups stock
← Back
Next step →
  • Screen stays awake for the whole session. This is the single most requested cook-mode behaviour.
  • Timers are detected from the words. "Simmer for 12 minutes" grows a 12:00 button inline. The user never types a duration.
  • Huge tap targets — Back and Next span half the screen each. Wet hands, no precision.
  • Only this step's ingredients are shown, so there is no scrolling back to the top mid-cook.
  • Finishing offers to tick the meal as eaten, closing the loop into the Dashboard.

You already have most of this

GuidedCooking.tsx exists and works. The upgrades are wake-lock, inline timer detection, per-step ingredients, and the hand-off back to the plan on completion.

The missing link

From a recipe you cannot reach a meal plan — the button is disabled and reads "Add to Meal Plan Coming Soon". The connection only runs the other way. Every concept on this board assumes that button works, because "I am looking at this recipe and I want to eat it on Thursday" is the most natural thought a person has in a recipe library.
